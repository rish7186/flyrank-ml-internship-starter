Can Trailing Search Trajectories and Query-Mix Signals Predict Content Decay Across Unseen Domains?

Rishabh Brahmane · November 2026 · Public-Safe Warehouse Evaluation

Abstract

This study investigates whether trailing search impression trajectories and 90-day query-mix concentration metrics can predict if an active web page will suffer a greater than 20% month-over-month drop in search impressions across unseen client domains. We analyzed 84,444 active content items (filtered from 519,606 cataloged URLs and 78.8M daily performance rows) across 104 pseudonymized clients in the FlyRank internship warehouse release. Using DuckDB SQL aggregations and a 200-tree Random Forest classifier trained on six pre-outcome features, we compared a standard stratified random split against a strict client-grouped split (GroupShuffleSplit on client_hash_id) to prevent domain-level leakage. While random splitting produced 0.705 accuracy (vs. a 0.637 majority base rate), holding out entire client domains reduced accuracy to 0.661 (below the 0.686 unseen-client base rate), though the model maintained 0.762 precision and 0.614 macro F1 compared to 0.407 for the majority baseline. Consequently, these signals are best deployed as a calibrated risk-ranking score to prioritize high-traffic pages for editorial refresh audits rather than as a standalone automated trigger.

1. Introduction & Decision Context

An SEO content strategist managing a portfolio of 10,000 published articles cannot manually audit every URL each month. By the time a monthly analytics report flags a sharp drop in organic visibility, rankings have often already eroded and recovery requires substantial editorial effort.

The obvious rule—flagging every page whose impressions dipped slightly last week—creates alert fatigue because normal search demand fluctuates daily. This work tests whether combining two months of prior impression volume with query-level concentration metrics (such as reliance on a single head query versus a broad query tail) provides an early-warning signal that holds up on brand-new client websites.

2. Data & Cohort Construction

All experiments were executed directly against the pseudonymized FlyRank/internship-warehouse Parquet release using DuckDB, querying column partitions remotely without downloading the full multi-gigabyte warehouse. Every entity is represented solely by cryptographic hashes (client_hash_id and content_hash_id), with zero domain names, client identities, or raw search query strings exposed.

  • dim_clients (104 clients): An unbalanced panel spanning 2025-01-27 to 2026-06-02, where onboarding depth varies substantially across accounts (only 4 clients carry 12 or more months of continuous Google Search Console history).
  • dim_content (519,606 content items): The full catalog of tracked content URLs mapped to their parent client hashes.
  • fact_content_daily_performance (78,835,655 rows): Daily search impressions, clicks, and average ranking position aggregated into three non-overlapping 30-day buckets across the trailing 90 days (imp_prev60, imp_prev30, and imp_last30).
  • fact_content_query_90d (2,414,248 rows): Page-level query diversity and concentration summaries over the 90-day window.

Exclusions & Volume Floor: To prevent minor daily noise on near-dormant pages from registering as false 20% declines, we required each page to record at least 200 impressions in the baseline window (imp_prev30 ≥ 200). This floor narrowed the catalog from 519,606 items to 88,301 active pages, and joining complete query-mix records yielded 84,444 modeled content items.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

Target Label: A content item is labeled as declining (is_declining = 1) when impressions in the final 30-day window drop by more than 20% relative to the prior 30 days (imp_last30 < 0.80 × imp_prev30), and stable or growing (0) otherwise. Across the modeled cohort, roughly two-thirds of active pages experienced a >20% month-over-month contraction during the observed window.

Features (6 Inputs): We excluded all same-window outcome metrics (imp_last30, clk_last30, pos_last30) and trained a 200-tree RandomForestClassifier (random_state=42) on six signals:

  • Impression Trajectory: imp_prev30 (impressions 31–60 days prior) and imp_prev60 (impressions 61–90 days prior).
  • Query Breadth & Concentration: visible_queries (distinct non-anonymized queries), rare_share (impression share from low-volume queries), anon_share (impression share from Google-anonymized queries), and top_query_share (ratio of top-query impressions to kept query impressions).

Leakage Checks & Split Comparison: Pages within the same website share domain-level algorithm shocks and seasonality. To test whether the model learns generalizable decay patterns rather than memorizing client-specific baselines, we evaluated identical models on a 75/25 Stratified Random Split (21,111 test rows) and a 75/25 Client-Grouped Split (GroupShuffleSplit on client_hash_id; 42,874 test rows across held-out clients with zero domain overlap), comparing both against a majority-class dummy baseline.

4. Results (Model vs. Baseline)

Evaluating on unseen client domains exposes a clear generalization gap: random row splitting overstates overall accuracy by 4.4 percentage points (0.705 vs. 0.661) compared to a strict client-grouped split. On unseen clients, the Random Forest trails the majority-class dummy baseline in raw accuracy (0.661 vs. 0.686 base rate) because predicting every page as declining exploits the high base rate, yet the Random Forest delivers substantially stronger class balance (0.614 vs. 0.407 macro F1) and higher precision (0.762 vs. 0.686).

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

These findings support directional editorial prioritization but cannot be treated as causal proof or deployed as an autonomous decision rule due to four constraints:

  • 90-Day Query-Table Window Overlap: fact_content_query_90d is a pre-aggregated trailing 90-day snapshot spanning both the historical baseline and the final 30-day outcome period. Consequently, query-mix features carry partial temporal overlap with the target window rather than strict point-in-time separation.
  • Cross-Domain Accuracy Ceiling & Unbalanced Panel: On unseen domains, overall accuracy (0.661) sits below the majority-class base rate (0.686). Because only 4 of 104 clients have 12+ months of search history, annual seasonal contractions cannot be distinguished from structural content decay.
  • Observational Signal Only: A measured >20% drop in impressions reflects visibility contraction, not its root cause (such as search engine core updates, competitor entrants, intentional URL pruning, or macro demand shifts).
  • Impressions-Only Scope: Excluding pages below 200 prior-30d impressions omits 431,305 long-tail URLs, and the target measures impression volume rather than clicks, leads, or revenue.

6. Ranked Recommendations

  1. Use Predicted Probabilities for Ranked Triage, Not Hard Thresholds: Sort active pages by predicted decline risk and focus human editorial audits on the top quartile (where precision reaches 0.762) rather than triggering automated changes from binary class labels.
  2. Mandate Client-Grouped Splits (GroupShuffleSplit) in All Benchmarks: Never rely on random row splits for multi-client SEO panels; enforce domain-level grouping so evaluation metrics reflect true out-of-sample generalization.
  3. Replace Trailing 90-Day Query Snapshots with Strictly Lagged Daily Features: Engineer point-in-time features strictly from days t-90 to t-31 in fact_content_daily_performance—such as average position slope, click-through rate drift, and daily impression volatility—to remove target-window overlap.
  4. Prioritize by Expected Impressions at Risk: Multiply predicted decline probability by baseline volume (p_decline × imp_prev30) so content teams address high-traffic pages before spending effort on low-visibility URLs.
